# Oedipus and the Sphinx (The Riddle of Man at the Crossroads of Thebes)

**Culture**: Greco-Roman | **Epoch**: -800 to -400
**Archetype**: Tragic Hero Solving the Monster's Enigma Ensnared by Unescapable Fate

## Scholarly Synopsis

Oedipus was a mythical Greek king of Thebes. A tragic hero in Greek mythology, Oedipus unwittingly fulfilled a prophecy that he would end up killing his father and marrying his mother, thereby bringing disaster to his city and family. The story of Oedipus is the subject of Sophocles's tragedy Oedipus Rex, which is followed in the narrative sequence by Oedipus at Colonus and then Antigone. Together, these plays make up Sophocles's three Theban plays. Oedipus represents two enduring themes of Greek myth and drama: the flawed nature of humanity and an individual's role in the course of destiny in a harsh universe.
